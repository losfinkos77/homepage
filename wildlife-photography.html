Field notes & settings

Wild animals rarely wait for you. These fundamentals help you get close (optically), stay unobtrusive and come home with sharp images.

Light first

The golden hour after sunrise and before sunset gives warm, soft light. Overcast days are ideal for even tones and fine detail. Avoid harsh midday sun and always expose for the highlights.

Camera settings

  • Birds in flight: 1/2000 s, continuous AF, burst mode
  • Perched birds: 1/500 s or faster
  • Mammals: 1/250–1/1000 s depending on movement
  • Aperture f/5.6–f/8 for sharpness, ISO as low as possible
  • Shoot RAW and use silent shutter when available

Fieldcraft

Move slowly and stop often. Use natural cover, avoid skylining yourself and keep conversations quiet. Patience beats chasing: find a promising spot, settle down and let the animals come to you.

Ethics first

  • Keep your distance – use a telephoto, not your feet
  • Never disturb nests, dens or young animals
  • No baiting or excessive playback for predators
  • Stay on trails and respect protected areas
  • No flash at night; leave no trace

Gear checklist

  • 300–600 mm telephoto (or 100–400 mm + teleconverter)
  • Monopod or bean bag for stability
  • Spare batteries and memory cards
  • Rain cover, lens cloth and binoculars
  • Quiet clothing that blends with the habitat

Time & places

Dawn and dusk are most active. Spring and autumn bring migration and courtship behaviour; after rain amphibians and insects appear. Local wetlands, forest edges and national parks are great starting points.
